# Initial uplift with Verus

Use for a requested uplift of an existing product. The deliverable is a readable account of consequential intent, a Verus foundation that distinguishes what that account promises, and evidence of where the running system agrees or differs. Pin the product and keep it unchanged. Inferred intent is provisional until accepted. Use the [verification playbook](verification-playbook.txt) as the working procedure; the G5 conventions below govern the human-facing outputs. The playbook’s construction and release steps are outside an uplift.

Use the product's actual concurrency and storage architecture. Playbook principle 3 describes database-centered services; for a queue, shared-memory library or another owner, model that mechanism's real contracts and record the differences and remaining premises instead of imposing database locks.

Apply shared [test-derived obligation recovery](../uplift-intent-recovery.md#recover-obligations-from-existing-tests) when recovering intent and checking formal coverage; reading or passing the tests alone does not close those obligations.

For a requested iterative loop, follow the shared [master orchestration](../uplift.md#when-an-iterative-loop-is-requested). TLC and Verus share the [iteration prompt](../uplift-iteration.md) and [scoring prompt](../uplift-scoring.md); use this guide and the playbook for Verus modeling and evidence.

Have an independent, read-only reviewer compare the specs with intent and the ontology at least once per phase, as required by playbook §§2 and 3.8. When claiming coverage through child blocks, apply the [parent coverage check](../uplift-review.md#coverage-through-child-blocks) and have the reviewer challenge that claim. Resolve each finding or record its disposition, and include the reviewer’s sign-off in the foundation handoff; the separate evaluator below measures performance and does not replace this review.

1. **Recover the product before committing to a proof family** (§§3.1, 4.1, 5a). Follow [G5 intent recovery](../uplift-intent-recovery.md): start from operator journeys, docs, tests, decisions, comments and fixes, then trace what actually decides the result. Survey the scoped production surface in both directions, from capabilities down and from underserved code back up. Enumerate relevant states × commands, writers, refusals, observers and provider boundaries. The size and variety of that surface determine the breadth of work; a few well-proved modules cannot stand in for the rest. Specify breadth first; mark unproved or unexplored cells and continue.
2. **Write the ontology once** (§3.1). Start at the root; put each complete rule, exception and observable result in its canonical [G5 block](../ontology-format.md). Make it possible to answer a consequential question from that page alone. Give the stable association to the relevant model or formal statement in block metadata. Maintain implementation links on the model side as described in the [foundation guide](setup_foundation.md). Generate any playbook index from these authorities; do not ask an author to maintain the same mapping twice.
3. **Represent and challenge the rule** (§§3.2–3.7, 4.2). Use [Verus verification](verification.md). Reuse the applicable shared models and actual provider contracts. A Python explorer or native probe may discover a defect or corroborate a result; if only that artifact distinguishes a material outcome, the Verus model is still missing it. Carry the distinguishing cases into Verus and retain the independent evidence. Derive the result from the actual deciding inputs and follow it to the consuming reader. For example, when children can override a parent default, hold the parent fixed and vary one child; a single shared-mode premise cannot decide that case.
4. **Use the gaps to select further work** (§§3.7–3.8, 5a). Apply the [iteration prompt](uplift-iteration.md) and the [scoring rules](uplift-scoring.md) to the current foundation. For independent comparison give the latter to a separate evaluator. Combine compatible inputs from an earlier uplift first when consolidation is requested: preserve both runnable trees, findings, domains, failing controls and receipts; reconcile contradictory claims against the same product. Distinguish newly recognized inherited evidence from new work. Close on the [foundation handoff](setup_foundation.md).

## Findings for the product owner

A discrepancy has different consequences depending on what is authoritative. Fix a misleading inferred statement in its owner. Preserve a confirmed requirement and record the product defect. If the evidence settles only what happens, record the competing intended outcomes in the [open questions register](../setup_foundation.md#open-questions). Search docs, history, discussions and native behavior before asking for a product decision. Record what would change under the alternative and continue independent work. Keep the governing statement accurate as findings change. Use the [shared findings format](setup_foundation.md#findings-and-questions) for issues and design simplifications. For a proposed product repair, name the behavior to preserve, the affected writers and readers, the case that distinguishes a safe change and whether the proposal itself was checked.

The bundled [playbook](verification-playbook.txt) is the upstream [983a56e snapshot](https://github.com/G5Labs/omnisaas/blob/983a56e5e1abfbcd17c69dee774f3416b4623645/playbook/verification-playbook.txt). Keep G5 adaptations in these guides so the upstream procedure can be refreshed independently.
