# Improve an existing uplift — builder prompt

Improve the given foundation at the pinned product revision. Use the selected [TLC uplift](TLC/uplift.md) or [Verus uplift](Verus/uplift.md) procedure and the shared [score](uplift-scoring.md) to choose work. For TLC, use its [model-checking mechanics](TLC/model-checking.md); for Verus, use [verification](Verus/verification.md) and the [playbook](Verus/verification-playbook.txt) (§§3.1–3.8, 4.2, 5a). This prompt describes one substantive batch; a requested continuous run is coordinated by the [master loop](uplift.md#when-an-iterative-loop-is-requested). Preserve a recoverable before image, the product, earlier good controls and the evaluator’s scoring rules. Use published feedback or compute a builder baseline if none exists. Do not inspect undisclosed evaluator cases.

1. Start from the last reconciled [census of ontology obligations](uplift-scoring.md#census-of-ontology-obligations), or establish it for the starting foundation if it is missing. Reuse current grades. Regrade obligations affected by changes to wording, models, premises or evidence, including parents that rely on a changed child. Choose work by consequence across the remaining obligations and workflows discovered independently from product sources. Group production members without active behavioral bindings, including citation-only members, by subsystem and workflow. Prioritize decisions that change an outcome, interactions, persistence, retries, failure and provider assumptions. Include an already-linked path where a different writer or final reader could invalidate the claimed coverage. Use released consequential gaps as named workflows in the existing queue. Also search independently from docs, history, tests and entry points. A large unfamiliar subsystem with few links deserves investigation even when the known panel is saturated.

   Use relevant existing test families to [recover obligations](uplift-intent-recovery.md#recover-obligations-from-existing-tests) and find gaps within these workflows, including already-linked ones. For a supported obligation, ask whether the formal model represents the test's conditions and excludes the outcome its assertion rejects. If not, identify the missing distinction or mismatched premise and address it in the owner, model or binding as appropriate. Reuse applicable fixtures and historical regressions as controls within the authorized execution scope. Keep test-derived cases distinct from independent evaluation cases; record predictions before inspecting the implementation or running the case, and disclose expectations already learned from tests. Continue investigating consequential behavior absent from tests.
2. For each promising workflow, predict two reachable neighboring outcomes from the owning ontology and from the formal model **before** inspecting the deciding implementation. Record the changing input or history, the reader and the expected difference in the existing queue. Follow that same case through the decision, effect and reader. When the claim assumes an in-scope producer’s result or stops before the reader, work on the connected missing roles together where feasible. Keep an external guarantee conditional where the provider cannot be verified. If the contract already distinguishes those cases, bind the missing source roles. If it cannot, extend the model, or recover and write missing intent in the canonical owner first. If a member delegates without changing the decision, justify the shared relation; if it is trivial for this scope, explain the exclusion. Keep unexamined members unassessed. Add a citation or model only for a specific source role or product distinction.
3. Check the distinguishing cases with the selected backend’s evidence rules. For a purported guarantee involving a provider, vary what that provider actually supplies. Check a valid neighbor and an altered rule the claim should reject. A native/Python finding belongs in the owner, formal specification and findings disposition as applicable; a probe-only claim remains unmodeled. Include a relevant safety, progress or recovery obligation when its absence changes the promised outcome; retain the backend’s bounds and assumptions. A result on a nearby workflow earns its own entry. Keep the original gap open until its specific contrast reaches its reader. At a partial checkpoint name the first missing decision, effect, reader or premise, and the observation that would resolve it. Refresh the affected bindings and receipts under the [TLC](TLC/setup_foundation.md#execution-evidence-and-reuse) or [Verus](Verus/setup_foundation.md#execution-evidence-and-reuse) reuse rules after the final relevant edit. Reconcile issues, simplifications and questions with the inherited entries, including what was withdrawn and why.
4. Reconcile the affected census entries after each batch and return the updated grades and remaining obligations in the handoff. Report newly reached members and members gaining an active binding, with their overlap and distinct total. Explain new locators, newly bound inherited behavior, new formal distinctions, and correspondence confirmed or withdrawn at the reader. In that report, name the material prediction gained or withdrawn, or the missing source role now bound for an existing prediction; say so when there is neither. Group variants that share a governing decision, final reader and fixture when they can be checked together. Keep cheap commits and proof receipts as checkpoints. After the batch, reconcile the product-wide inventory with the integrated artifacts: distinguish the completed slice from its residual workflows, and choose the next material gap. In a requested master loop, return this recommendation to the master; finishing an assignment does not stop the loop. Move to another underserved workflow when it has more consequential unresolved behavior than another variant of the current one. Scale the number of areas and depth to the product, not a fixed number of models, rounds or minutes.
5. When recent work stops changing a material prediction, challenge that judgment on the strongest remaining candidate and a tractable workflow outside the recently investigated family. Choose from the builder’s inventory, product tests, docs or entry points. For each, check what the canonical owner predicts, what the formal model determines and which production roles are bound. When a runtime probe confirms a consequential outcome, identify the formal claim that would reject the wrong outcome. If the model permits both, keep the gap in the queue. Use the fixture to model and bind the distinction when that is the highest-value work, or record why it can wait.

   Finishing a family returns the run to the product-wide inventory. Before declaring convergence across the requested scope, show where unlinked work remains and how the weakly linked or unassessed areas were judged. Where an area could still contain a consequential decision, inspect a representative public workflow to decide whether further work would improve the ontology, formal claim or binding. Continue with the strongest tractable gap. A declining rate of new bugs supports a claim about bug discovery in the families examined. Claim convergence only for the scope the evidence supports. If scope, cost or a blocker ends the run with material areas unresolved, name them, the reason for stopping and the cost of settling the largest unknown. No raw percentage, stable headline panel or successful proof establishes convergence.

Hand off the candidate after image, changed canonical owners, active formal claims, source-binding delta, registers, controls, receipts, residual queue, cost and independent review status. A delegated worker returns after its assigned batch with recommendations for further work; the master integrates it, schedules the required review and decides overall convergence. For a standalone iteration, complete the required independent review before calling the foundation sealed. Report discoveries from this search separately from previously known repairs. Keep every sampled workflow and its prediction in the handoff, including misses and candidates set aside. Let the independent scorer determine performance on its own fresh cases.
