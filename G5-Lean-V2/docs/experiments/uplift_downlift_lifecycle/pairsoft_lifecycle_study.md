# PairSoft uplift/downlift lifecycle: procedure and lessons

Research retrospective · 11 October 2026. Based on repository documents, selected source/model/proof inspection, Git snapshots, and the original Codex and Claude Code conversations. Reported verification results are historical evidence; this study did not rerun the campaigns. Skill changes are recommendations, not implemented here.

## Goal

Turn a complex existing product into an understandable, maintainable system whose important behavior survives modernization. Recover the intent, expose contradictions against actual implementation, choose an explicitly scoped simpler design, implement it, and keep its guarantees intact as features return. Ontology carries the enduring intent and decisions between these activities; models, proofs and tests provide different kinds of evidence about them.

PairSoft demonstrates this loop, with qualifications: its first uplift inherited substantial old ontology; its replacement deliberately omitted features and changed some policies; and the resulting backend has local verification evidence, not demonstrated full product parity or qualified production operation. Rust, Verus, a rewrite and a modular monolith were choices for this experiment, not universal lifecycle requirements.

## What happened

### 1. Recover and reorganize intent — September 22 onward

The initial Codex task compressed the old `.g5` ontology and `docs/knowledge`, preserving important human commitments and agent decisions that code alone could not explain. Human feedback corrected the first result: workflows and data concepts were insufficiently represented, and separate “decisions” and “knowledge” buckets obscured their semantic owners. The ontology was reorganized around workflows, data, invariants, security and architecture.

This was initially **migration and consolidation of existing knowledge**, not a complete fresh reconciliation of documentary claims with current code. The session explicitly distinguished preserving draft observations from re-verifying them. Moreover, the knowledge articles described themselves as AI drafts/checklists; their assertions needed support from original functional designs, decision logs, ADRs and implementation. Later audits supplied much of that reconciliation. [Initial conversation](</Users/ziniuw/.codex/sessions/2026/09/22/rollout-2026-09-22T17-22-12-01a0caff-6db8-7a80-81d7-2138c420e1d5.jsonl>), [source inventory and coverage review](../ontology_design_principle_enforcement/pairsoft/ontology-coverage-review.md).

For a normal repository without old ontology, begin with those original records, code and relevant Git history. Extract commitments with their conditions, exceptions and authority before compressing them. Use history to explain disputed behavior and supersession; current implementation is evidence of behavior, not automatic evidence of intended behavior. An existing ontology is another source to reconcile, not a prerequisite.

### 2. Check the recovered contracts against the existing system — October 1–3

The verification campaign pinned the original product to `main b9613c09` and PairSoftCore 2.0.10. Models followed actual guards, event handlers, persistence boundaries and failure points. As-built behavior and proposed repairs were kept distinct. For example, requisition approval completion checked that the requisition was awaiting approval, but did not establish that the completion belonged to its current submission; the model made that missing identity explicit.

The first selection emphasized risky mechanisms. The human then challenged its comprehensiveness, leading to a feature denominator: **65 features, with 39 covered, 16 partly covered, four planned and six excluded**. Source reconciliation also exposed contradictions, missing rules and working assumptions. These dispositions mattered more than a large count of passing model checks.

Runtime reproduction became a second research instrument. The final handoff classified 110 findings as 77 reproduced, 29 partly reproduced, two not reproduced/latent and two code-only findings not reproducible locally. Many originated in code/specification analysis rather than a solver counterexample. Real execution corrected models and supplied additional findings; actual natural keys, tracking behavior and handler registration narrowed some initial claims. In-process services, real SQL Server and external stand-ins still left deployment and provider behavior outside the evidence. [Coverage registry](../ontology_design_principle_enforcement/pairsoft/coverage-registry.md), [verification handoff](../ontology_design_principle_enforcement/pairsoft/verification-handoff.md), [runtime test assessment](../ontology_design_principle_enforcement/pairsoft/trajectory-test-assessment.md).

The resulting ontology amendments strengthened identities, authorization, ordering, freshness, atomicity and recovery contracts. They did not authorize silently replacing unresolved legacy policies with a preferred new policy. Later ontology commit `c4abdb3b` also contained newer expense/change-order contracts than the pinned verification baseline; the two snapshots must remain distinguishable.

### 3. Choose and check a smaller target design — October 3

The human explicitly requested a separate Rust backend with no UI, incomplete feature coverage, clean abstractions and a feasible Verus proof boundary. A full feature catalog preserved the denominator while a **new target ontology** specified the selected core. Its initial catalog classified the 65 baseline features as ten full, 36 partial and 19 later, with 17 additional entries from newer ontology. These were scope dispositions, not evidence that implementation was finished.

The initial architecture was a modular monolith: one purchase owns its business lifecycle; synchronous private Rust state implements decisions; an application coordinator and adapters handle effects; a tenant PostgreSQL lock serializes commits. State, command result, audit and outgoing intent commit together. External uncertainty still requires durable ingress, stable operation identities and recovery. This removed much internal coordination, while accepting tenant-level throughput constraints.

Requester-only editing, a fixed independent reviewer quorum, immutable issued orders and restricted purchase dimensions were **new scope/policy decisions**. Their simplicity cannot be credited entirely to better architecture or claimed as preservation of every legacy capability. The catalog retained deferred capabilities and their attachment points. [Design handoff](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/design_handoff.md>), [design review](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/design_review.md>), [feature catalog](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/full_feature_catalog.md>).

Six initial concern models—Approval, Fulfilment, Effects, Ingress, Sync and Restore—then exposed seven design defects. Concrete alternatives produced useful simplifications:

| Problem exposed by modeling | Chosen simplification and its cost |
|---|---|
| A manager could edit and then approve their own changes | Requester-only editing avoided maintaining editor history; this narrowed policy. |
| A replacement receipt export could overtake its reversal | FIFO effects per purchase replaced per-job dependency relationships; unrelated work within that purchase can wait. |
| A tenant fence could prevent claimed work from finishing | Fence new work while permitting correlated completion; derive unknown outcome from lease time instead of adding a sweeper writer. |
| Delayed events or snapshots could resurrect deleted reference data | Reuse the synchronization watermark to reject stale input; the first repair failed because it guarded events but not snapshots. |
| Restore/retry could create another external operation | Derive stable business operation keys and reconcile before retry; safe completion still depends on explicit provider guarantees or qualified evidence. |

The repaired bounded models checked all 44 initial properties. TLC helped reject flawed repairs and compare particular mechanisms. The broader architecture had already been chosen through design reasoning; neither fewer explored states nor passing checks proves it globally minimal. [Initial model findings and alternatives](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/TLA_on_initial_design/findings.md>).

### 4. Implement, test and extend the proof boundary — October 3–11

Implementation followed the target ontology in `PairSoftPlatform_simplified`, with one continuing plan. Full business-kernel Verus proof was explicitly deferred during an early implementation stage, then separately authorized. Local provider simulators enabled progress under documented assumptions while live service qualification remained separate. PairSoftCore source established some transport/data contracts, but did not establish every ERP deduplication, lookup or retention guarantee. [Implementation plan](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/implementation_plan.md>), [service evidence](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/contracts/service-evidence.md>).

Verification repeatedly changed the implementation and its specification:

- **Runtime correspondence:** indexed model trajectories became API/worker scenarios with independent database, journal and provider observations. Real PostgreSQL, multiple processes, controlled lock ordering, lost COMMIT acknowledgments and restore failures exercised boundaries absent from unit tests. Protected counterexamples ended at the first correct refusal; impossible suffixes were not presented as executable coverage. The indexed traces were finite correspondence evidence, not formal refinement.
- **Kernel proofs:** Verus checked production business functions against explicit invariants, guards, effects and frames. Writing these specifications exposed missing history, identities and input premises before proof execution. A successful preservation proof did not by itself establish that all valid commands were accepted, all required notifications occurred, or unverified adapters supplied complete inputs.
- **Boundary verification:** later work checked loaders, persistence, all writers, lock ordering, declared decision reads, trusted helpers and SQL effects. It found mixed invoice working sets, upserts overwriting collisions, missing-row updates and decisions made outside the kernel. Compositional SQL/commit proofs gained structural and runtime bindings to handlers; they did not turn the complete asynchronous Rust/SQL stack into one source-level Verus proof.
- **Specification adequacy:** an adversarial comparison with ontology found unsafe retry and recovery behavior despite passing proofs. Real backup/restore exploration then found lost automatic vendor mail: code and models shared an open-tenant assumption and omitted the needed progress obligation. A subsequent settings-change case exposed a second stall. Proof-oriented refactoring also caused a lock-duration performance regression, found by measurement.

By the October 11 boundary milestone, recorded results included **652 kernel proof obligations**, **25 TLA+ models/329 expected check outcomes**, and **214 passing tests with five ignored**. These measure different scopes and must not be added into a “percent verified.” Whole-system eventual completion remained bounded exploration with environmental premises. Real providers and customer operation were unqualified; the verification manifest still explicitly said `production_ready: false`. [Runtime correspondence](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/evidence/composed-coverage.md>), [Verus findings](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/verus/verification_findings_and_status.md>), [October 9–11 boundary findings](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/verification_session_2026-10-09_to_11.md>), [verification manifest](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/evidence/verification.json>).

### 5. Add features and revisit the guarantees — overlapping October 4–11

There were two distinct tracks. Experiments added staged approvals, buyer-center operations, expenses and change orders on isolated comparison branches; they were not successive completed additions to the main product. A separate merged backend-gap effort added tax, vendor documents/mail, receiving drafts, richer master data, invoices and other backend capabilities. It remained narrower than the original product.

Growth was a practical test of modularity. Some additions reused the existing job/recovery lifecycle and required less new state; others exposed invalid reuse assumptions, stale facts at commit, or missing cumulative correction bounds. The backend expansion also broke the previously working Verus build, and later proof work found new defects. A verified starting point did not preserve verification automatically.

The comparison experiments do not establish universal G5 superiority or overhead. Plain branches inherited the simplified architecture and some verification infrastructure; execution conditions varied. Several pairs reached similar architectures, and repeated unchanged model suites sometimes cost much more than checking the new mechanism. The useful result is a set of failure modes and cost controls, not a causal productivity percentage. [Experiment takeaways](../ontology_design_principle_enforcement/pairsoft-harness-experiment-takeaways.md), [backend gap backlog](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/backend_functional_gap_backlog.md>), [proof gap-closure record](</Users/ziniuw/Desktop/customers/PairSoft/PairSoftPlatform_simplified/docs/verus/gap_closure_fixes.md>).

## Generalizable procedure

Keep the lifecycle in the existing plan and ontology. Each transition needs a clear result, not another mandatory document.

| Activity | Result needed before dependent work |
|---|---|
| Recover intent | Source-derived feature inventory and concise commitments, including exceptions, authority, contradictions and unknowns; code/history reconciliation identifies implemented behavior separately. |
| Diagnose the existing system | Risk-directed models bound to actual operations and failure boundaries; executable reproduction where feasible; each discrepancy classified as code, intent, model or binding error. Coverage dispositions prevent a narrow campaign being called complete. |
| Select the target | Explicit retained/changed/deferred scope, old-to-new semantic ownership, external contracts and migration/coexistence choice. Compare simpler alternatives against the same required outcomes before attributing gains to design. |
| Check and implement | For downlift, cover every retained original core feature with concern models; implement complete operations and recovery paths from the reviewed target. Preserve counterexamples, permitted completion and independent runtime assertions. |
| Establish assurance | State exactly what models, code proofs and tests establish, on which revision and assumptions. Verify the load–decide–persist boundary and competing writers; qualify external behavior and operating limits separately. |
| Grow incrementally | Amend the same governing design when needed; inspect actual reused operations and every consumer of new obligations, including drain, restore and retirement. Refresh affected contracts, tests and existing proofs. Use incremental TLA+/TLC when explicitly requested under the current workflow. |

For arbitrary repositories, select an in-place refactor, staged replacement or rewrite according to their constraints. PairSoft started the new core with new purchases rather than demonstrating migration of active legacy purchases. A real modernization must explicitly cover data conversion, compatibility, ownership during coexistence, cutover and rollback whenever those are in scope. Choose a provable core and suitable tooling where valuable; unavailable whole-system proof should narrow the assurance claim, not stop useful engineering.

## Lessons for the skills

The current [uplift](../../../.agents/skills/ontology-uplift/SKILL.md), [downlift](../../../.agents/skills/ontology-downlift/SKILL.md) and [model-checking guide](../../../.agents/skills/ontology-planning/references/model-checking.md) already incorporate much of this experience: source-first recovery, independent intent/implementation status, original-feature denominators, as-built versus repaired models, simpler alternatives, reachability and negative controls, trajectory tests, assumptions and evidence reuse. Preserve those rules; avoid adding a second lifecycle wrapper that duplicates them.

The most useful refinements are:

1. **Make the transition from recovered system to target system explicit.** Carry source baselines, feature dispositions, retained exceptions and changed policies into the existing downlift plan. Link semantic owners across ontologies without copying the old implementation structure. Add a migration/coexistence disposition; PairSoft's new-purchase restriction must not become an invisible default.
2. **Design the assurance boundary early.** Identify complete input reads, current facts required at commit, output writes and alternative writers alongside the kernel. Later PairSoft work repeatedly discovered that a sound function was surrounded by unsound loading or storage. Introduce cheap ownership/read/lock checks when the chosen architecture makes them useful, with injected violations that prove the checks work.
3. **Check what specifications omit.** For consequential operations, cover legitimate acceptance, correct refusal, required effects, unchanged state and progress under explicit environmental assumptions. Compare contracts back to human intent during normal implementation; use the separate review workflow only when requested. Lost mail and unsafe retries show why code and its model must not serve as each other's sole oracle.
4. **Carry new obligations through the full lifecycle.** Submission, correction, close, pause, crash, restore, retirement and late replies can interact even when each path passes in isolation. Test combinations against real persistence and independently observed effects. Preserve trusted-code tests and measure performance after changes made for provability.
5. **Make verification survive feature growth.** Track the existing proof/check obligations affected by changed owners, fields, reads and writes. Where the project requires them, automate revision-bound checks so a feature cannot silently leave its proof build broken. Keep proof status, feature completeness, integration qualification and operational readiness separate; a kernel flag cannot summarize all four.
6. **Keep evidence current and proportionate.** Update the owning status when work supersedes it. PairSoft's early catalog/plan still describes deferred full proofs while later records establish them; even the generic manifest's hosted-CI flag differs from the later session's cited runs. Retain historical scope, identify current evidence and explain differences. Reuse unchanged results only when code, model, configuration and relevant premises still match. Prefer targeted checks over repeated broad campaigns, without introducing a large evidence-management framework.

The strongest observed benefit came from making intent, identities, state ownership and boundary contracts precise. Counterexamples, proof failures and runtime tests then challenged different parts of that precision. Much defect discovery happened while constructing specifications; the records do not support a general numerical split between “reasoning” and “solver” value, nor a universal return on formal methods.

The subsequent Twenty uplift tested recovery **without prior ontology** in [the initial chat](codex://threads/01a12c44-9742-7600-b495-3e68bcdfbb9b) and [its continuation](codex://threads/01a12d47-7ab3-79e0-a9aa-5981625f1b4d). Human follow-ups drove broader discovery, code/model correspondence checks, readable parent/child contracts and complete question reconciliation. Later audits still found major capabilities, and model links did not establish whole-feature coverage. The [current report](/Users/ziniuw/Desktop/projects/G5-benchmark/twenty/.g5/uplift/backend-2026-10-11/code-reproduction/README.md) withdraws earlier completeness claims. This tests uplift; it does not yet validate Twenty downlift or isolate the skill's causal contribution.

The refined harness therefore uses five phases: indexed intent recovery; joint ontology/model development; executed checks and gap reconciliation; independent reconstruction-coverage audit; final human-readable refinement. Before extraction, the human confirms proposed external sources, their rough authority and scope; backend-only is the default, with explicit frontend inclusion/exclusion when present. Authority follows actual decisions, corrections and context, not merely source type or confident wording. Uncertain intent retains Q with a justified provisional option and conditional results. Completion requires broad direct source investigation and meaningful checks of important behavioral obligations; model counts and linked files cannot substitute. Known implementation gaps remain separate from ontology completeness. Future manual tests should evaluate missed commitments, reconstruction fidelity and actual verification outcomes, including the cost of reaching those checkpoints.

Completeness also needs a reconstruction challenge: derive major flows from ontology and compare them with expectations gathered separately from records and implementation. Ask whether materially wrong behavior could satisfy every written commitment; resolve evidence-backed differences as omissions, uncertain policy or permitted design freedom. Preserve important rules even when local or easily recovered from code, while omitting incidental mechanisms. TLC checks selected models and properties, so model/action coverage cannot reveal requirements absent from both. The result is a supported completeness claim within a declared scope and evidence boundary, not a percentage of all historical intent. The harness records these challenges in existing coverage notes rather than adding another artifact or mandatory agent workflow.
