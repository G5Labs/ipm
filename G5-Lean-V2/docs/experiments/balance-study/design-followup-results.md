## What this supports

The earlier omission was tractable through more accurate, scoped knowledge. Success
with ordinary prose also makes block syntax an unsupported explanation for this gain.
The fact packet was short and unusually salient; it was not equal-information or
equal-retrieval-cost treatment against the complete ontology. This case motivated the
repair, so these are fresh-context regression checks, not held-out generalization or a
reliability estimate.

The heavy workflow remains useful for making proposed contractual changes visible to a
human. Its artifacts do not by themselves prove better decisions: the original H trial
put its base restriction in ontology even while the plan sounded compatible. Inspecting
the two artifacts together was decisive. The repaired H trial consistently preserves
the established positive exception.

## Evaluator clarification: proposed policy versus recovered fact

The frozen follow-up criterion warns against inventing an exclusive administrator-only
base-transfer rule because the original general owner grant leaves that case unresolved.
H-r1 says an ordinary owner cannot use immediate transfer, presenting this under proposed
choices. The original task explicitly allowed reasonable product policy choices. We
therefore record this as a consequential **new policy proposal**, requiring human design
review, rather than pretending source proves it false. Its rationale about avoiding a
second path does not independently establish pre-existing intent. The primary positive
administrator/base exception passes. This clarification preserves the original criterion
and avoids turning evaluator uncertainty into a mandatory prohibition.
