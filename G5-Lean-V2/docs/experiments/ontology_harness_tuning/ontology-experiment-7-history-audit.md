# Experiment 7: audit of both workers' histories

Date: 2026-09-27. Experiment: **Fork & Start**. Conditions: ontology with planning/review harness, and plain with a generic review request.

## Findings in brief

**The main problem was incomplete translation of cross-module contracts into implementation and discriminating checks.** The ontology worker read relevant Stop, consent and delivery rules, preserved a design, and repaired several real defects during review. It still missed interactions between the new coordinator and existing public operations. The plain worker independently found several of the same classes of problem, but also made an incorrect authorization decision and reused an incompatible replay identity.

I would prioritize **better boundary verification and review evidence**, followed by a few precise ontology clarifications. Adding more general reminders to “respect ownership,” “handle races” or “review carefully” is unlikely to resolve these failures: both histories already contain those intentions.

The **3.1/10** ontology result is a strict obligation/evidence score, not a calibrated general code-quality grade. It represents 2.5 points across eight broad obligations. The plain result was 1.9/10. The scores legitimately flag incomplete delivery, but conceal useful repairs: ontology improved from **10/13 to 11/13** common obligation probes; plain improved from **8/13 to 9/13**. A remaining failure can keep a whole obligation at zero, and the Stop defect affects two obligations. These qualifications do not excuse the remaining defects.

The tables below contain **33 observations**, including remaining defects, repaired mistakes, verification weaknesses and evaluator problems. They are not 33 independent production bugs. Related observations are explicitly connected.

### How to read the classifications

**Ontology** means improving the authoritative product or architecture contract. **Harness** means improving how an agent discovers, implements or demonstrates that contract, including experiment infrastructure. “Can resolve” describes a plausible targeted intervention, not a measured causal effect. **Residual difficulty** names what remains hard even with those improvements. Confidence is high for observed actions and retained test results; recommendations and causal explanations are judgments.

## What remains genuinely difficult?

There is **no evidence here that any specific requirement is inherently too hard for an agent regardless of ontology or harness**. There are difficult engineering problems whose reliability cannot be guaranteed by better prose alone:

| Difficult class | Evidence in this experiment | What helps | What remains hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordering effects across SQL commits, fork/Git publication, VM commands and API restarts | B3, B8, B9 and C4; both workers built durable coordination but repaired missing boundaries late. | Explicit identities, owner-mediated atomic checks, durable recovery obligations, deterministic barriers and crash injection. | Choosing representative schedules, ensuring recovery is complete, and avoiding deadlocks or stale effects across all schedules. |
| Combining operations with different authority and effect scopes | A1, A2, A4, A5, A6. | Canonical allowed/forbidden scenarios, predicates checked at the effect owner, and tests involving existing independent work. | Recognizing interactions outside the new coordinator. These particular misses were nevertheless expressible as small, feasible counterexamples. |
| Delivery uncertainty and truthful user-visible progress across independent state owners | A3, B6 and C5. | Explicit accepted/queued/uncertain/running distinctions, stable identities, projection from authoritative records, controlled lost-response tests. | Establishing real provider acceptance under transport failure and maintaining a truthful UI through partial observations. A scripted ready VM cannot establish that. |

The histories show recoverable omissions and incorrect assumptions, not a demonstrated ceiling on agent understanding. They also show that **more time spent on review is insufficient unless review asks discriminating questions**: both workers spent roughly 14–16 minutes in their recorded review intervals, yet the remaining common failures survived.

## Recommended changes, in priority order

These are proposals for a later harness/ontology change; this audit does not modify either.

| Priority | Change | Where it belongs | How to tell whether it helped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | For each reused owner guarantee, trace one real public operation through its consumer; include early returns and a case with unrelated existing work. | Planning/review harness; examples attached to lifecycle and composition contracts. | A future run catches queued public Stop and operation cancellation affecting another chat before final delivery. Direct-row generation tests alone do not qualify. |
| **2** | Maintain a concise contract-to-evidence table covering identities, authority, consent deadline, predecessor retirement, cancellation scope and public progress. Record the actual enforcement point. | Saved design and post-implementation review. | Every consequential commitment has implementation evidence plus a discriminating check or an explicit unverified gap. |
| **3** | Clarify four product distinctions: source read versus child ownership; operation kind in replay identity; operation cancellation versus workspace Stop; allocation versus delivery/running. | Canonical workspace/fork, lifecycle and conversation/progress ontology blocks, with links rather than duplication. | Positive reader compatibility, cross-operation key collision, unrelated-chat cancellation and progress-state cases are designed without evaluator hints. |
| **4** | Couple every new denial or guard with the nearest allowed case, and every retry identity with its predecessor/old-operation case. | Review harness and authorization/replay fixtures. | Hardening cannot silently remove the valid reader flow; old Fork-only requests cannot acquire new effects. |
| **5** | Provide deterministic time, pause points and restart fixtures at actual owner boundaries; preflight a usable VM/provider/browser runner when full outcome evidence is required. | Test infrastructure and experiment setup. | Separate results exist for simulated readiness, real execution, concurrent owner changes and abrupt restart. Missing infrastructure is known before dispatch. |
| **6** | Make review and evaluation evidence precise: no stand-in completion labels, coarse percentages or premature completion receipts; robust history export and revision-specific builds. | Review reporting and experiment tooling. | Reports distinguish repaired defects, remaining defects, source-only risks and unknowns; measurements can be reproduced from the frozen revisions. |

The strongest next experiment would test these targeted improvements with the same level of supervision and a new feature or held-out cases. Feeding these exact failures to a rerun would measure assisted repair, which is useful but answers a different question from autonomous cross-module implementation.
